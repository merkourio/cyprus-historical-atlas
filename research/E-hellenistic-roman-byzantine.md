## `hellenistic` — Hellenistic (323–58 BCE), power: Ptolemaic Egypt

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "Origin: Macedonian dynasty ruling from Alexandria" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ptolemaic-dynasty
- CLAIM: "Seat: Salamis, then Nea Paphos as capital of the strategos" — CONFIRMED. Pelops, the first securely attested strategos (from 217 BCE), was based at Salamis; the seat moved to Nea Paphos early in the 2nd century BCE. Source: https://paphos-agora.archeo.uj.edu.pl/en_GB/nea-pafos
- CLAIM: "for two and a half centuries Cyprus was Egypt's timber yard, copper mine, grain store and naval base. A Ptolemaic governor ruled from Nea Paphos" — CONFIRMED (Britannica: "For two and a half centuries, Cyprus remained a Ptolemaic possession, ruled by a strategus, or governor-general"; Paphos Agora Project: navy base, cedar timber, mercenary garrison). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus ; https://paphos-agora.archeo.uj.edu.pl/en_GB/nea-pafos
- CLAIM: "Greek became the only public language, and the old syllabary died out" — CORRECTED: "Greek koine became the language of government, and the old Cypriot syllabary faded out by the end of the third century BCE." Reason: the Kafizin dedications (225–218 BCE) are still partly syllabic, and Phoenician lingered at Kition into the early Hellenistic period, so "only" is too strong without a date. Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/inscriptions/alphabetic-inscriptions-of-cyprus-epigraphic-contribution-to-reconstruction-of-history-of-ancient-cyprus ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/abs/kafizin-and-the-cypriot-syllabary/9DCB299AD7B2ED38D0E2A8F4D65E3C46
- CLAIM: "321 BCE: Ptolemy I allies with the Cypriot kings against Antigonus" — CORRECTED: "321 BCE: Four Cypriot kings (Salamis, Soli, Paphos, Amathus) side with Ptolemy I against the regent Perdiccas." Reason: in 321/320 the war was against Perdiccas; Antigonus was then Ptolemy's ally (Antipater sent him to help the Cypriot kings). Antigonus only became the enemy from 315, when Kition, Marion, Lapethos and Keryneia went over to him. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicocles_of_Paphos ; https://www.attalus.org/names/c/cyprus.html (entry 320/6, Arrian, Events after Alexander) ; https://cyprustravels.org/history/hellenistic-period/
- CLAIM: "312 BCE: Ptolemy abolishes the city-kingdoms; Nikokreon of Salamis dies" — CORRECTED (split into two events): "312 BCE: Ptolemy deposes the pro-Antigonid kings of Kition, Marion, Lapethos and Keryneia and makes Nikokreon of Salamis governor of the whole island" and "c. 310 BCE: Nikokreon dies (suicide or murder); Ptolemy's brother Menelaos takes over as governor." Reason: Nikokreon died 311/310, not 312, and the kingdoms of Salamis, Soli, Paphos and Amathus survived a little longer (Nicocles of Paphos was forced to suicide c. 310; Menelaos even bore the title king of Salamis). Some scholars think Diodorus' Nicocles story is really about Nikokreon. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ptolemaic_governors_of_Cyprus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicocreon_of_Cyprus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_I_Soter ; http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0006%3Aentry%3Dsalamis-1
- CLAIM: "306 BCE: Demetrius Poliorcetes wins the naval battle of Salamis; the Antigonids hold Cyprus" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Salamis_(306_BC) ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/cyprus/cyprus-4/
- CLAIM: "294 BCE: Ptolemaic rule restored for good" — CONFIRMED (Ptolemy reconquered Cyprus 295/294; Livius simplifies to "after Ipsus", but Demetrius kept the island until 295/294). Suggest "295/294 BCE". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_I_Soter ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/cyprus/cyprus-4/
- CLAIM: "200 BCE: Nea Paphos becomes the seat of the Ptolemaic governor" — CONFIRMED as "c. 200 BCE" (Jagiellonian project: "from ca. 200 BC to ca. AD 350" Nea Paphos was capital; transfer "early in the 2nd century BCE"). Write "c. 200 BCE". Source: https://paphos-agora.archeo.uj.edu.pl/en_GB/nea-pafos
- CLAIM (region context): "Seleucids and Ptolemies fight six Syrian Wars. Rome defeats Carthage and enters the Greek East." — CONFIRMED (six Syrian Wars 274–168 BCE; the Sixth ends with Rome's ultimatum at Eleusis, 168). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Wars ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gaius-Popillius-Laenas
- LABEL: "31, 29 | Ptolemaic Egypt" — CONFIRMED (Middle Egypt, on Ptolemaic core). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ptolemaic-dynasty
- LABEL: "38, 36.6 | Seleucid Empire" — CONFIRMED (northern Syria near Gaziantep, Seleucid heartland; Seleucid state lasts to 64 BCE, so valid for the whole era). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Wars
- LABEL: "22.5, 40.7 | Antigonid Macedon" — CORRECTED: "Macedon (Antigonid 276–168 BCE)". Reason: point is right (Pella/Thessaloniki region) but Antigonid kings hold Macedon only from 276 (Antigonus II Gonatas) to Pydna 168; before that Cassander's Antipatrids, after 168 Rome. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigonid_dynasty
- LABEL: "28.2, 36.4 | Rhodes" — CONFIRMED (island of Rhodes; independent trading republic throughout the era). Source: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/cyprus/cyprus-4/
- LABEL: "35.5, 31.9 | Judaea" — CORRECTED: "Judaea (Hasmonean from c. 140 BCE)". Reason: Judaea was Ptolemaic to 200, Seleucid to the 140s, then Hasmonean kingdom; the coordinate (Judaean hills near Jerusalem) is right. Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hasmonean-dynasty

## `roman` — Roman (58 BCE–330 CE), power: Roman Empire

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "Origin: Rome, as a senatorial province under a proconsul" — CONFIRMED for 22 BCE onward; before that Cyprus was run with Cilicia (58–47 BCE), returned to the Ptolemies (47–30 BCE), then an imperial province (30/27–22 BCE). Suggest: "Rome; from 22 BCE a senatorial province under a proconsul." Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "Seat: Nea Paphos, later Salamis" — CORRECTED: "Nea Paphos (Salamis-Constantia only after the earthquakes of the 340s)". Reason: Salamis was refounded as Constantia and made capital c. 346 under Constantius II — after this era's 330 end-date. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cyprus ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/salamis-cyprus/
- CLAIM: "Annexed in 58 BCE, Cyprus was a quiet, wealthy province of theatres, baths and villas" — CONFIRMED (Britannica: 600 years of peace, gymnasium and theatre at Salamis, theatre at Kourion, governor's palace at Paphos). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus
- CLAIM: "Paul and Barnabas preached here in 45 CE and the island claims the first Christian governor" — CONFIRMED as "c. 45 CE" (Britannica "about 45 ce"; Acts 13 says the proconsul Sergius Paulus "believed"; inscriptions confirm a proconsul Paulus but not the year, so "claims" is the right register). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergius_Paulus
- CLAIM: "Earthquakes in the fourth century devastated the cities, and recovery moved the capital to Salamis, rebuilt as Constantia" — CONFIRMED in substance but chronologically belongs to the Byzantine era (332/342 quakes, Constantia c. 346). Suggest moving this sentence to `byzantine` or phrasing "In the 340s, just after our era closes, ...". Source: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/salamis-cyprus/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "58 BCE: Cato the Younger annexes Cyprus for Rome" — CONFIRMED (lex Clodia de Cypro, 58 BCE; Cato carried it out, king Ptolemy of Cyprus killed himself). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_of_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "22 BCE: Cyprus becomes a senatorial province under a proconsul" — CONFIRMED (Britannica: "became a separate 'senatorial' province in 22 bce"). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus
- CLAIM: "45 CE: Paul and Barnabas preach at Salamis and Paphos" — CONFIRMED as "c. 45 CE" (scholars range 45–47; Acts 13:4–12). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-Barnabas
- CLAIM: "116 CE: A Jewish revolt devastates Salamis and is crushed" — CONFIRMED (part of the Diaspora Revolt 115–117; Britannica: suppressed "by a lieutenant of the future emperor Hadrian in 116 ce"; Eusebius/Orosius on the destruction of Salamis; Jews afterwards banned from the island). Suggest "115–117 CE". Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus ; https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/artemion ; https://www.judaism-and-rome.org/epitaph-soldier-who-fought-jewish-revolt-cyprus-ae-1992-1689
- CLAIM: "332 CE: The first of a series of catastrophic earthquakes" — UNCERTAIN on the year, and it sits after the era's 330 end-date. The 332 and 342 quakes come from later chronicles (Livius repeats "332 and 342"); the archaeologically secure destructions are 342 and 365–370. Suggest moving to `byzantine` as "332/342 CE: Earthquakes wreck Salamis, refounded as Constantia by Constantius II". Source: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/salamis-cyprus/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cyprus
- CLAIM (region context): "The Roman Republic becomes an empire under Augustus. Christianity spreads through the eastern provinces. Crisis and recovery in the third century." — CONFIRMED (general). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/history-of-Cyprus
- LABEL: "37, 35.2 | Roman Syria" — CONFIRMED (central Syria near Hama; Roman from 64 BCE). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Palaestina
- LABEL: "31, 29 | Roman Egypt" — CONFIRMED for 30 BCE onward; Ptolemaic for the first 28 years of the era. Suggest "Egypt (Roman from 30 BCE)". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cyprus
- LABEL: "28, 38.6 | Roman Asia" — CONFIRMED (Lydia, province of Asia). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attalid_dynasty
- LABEL: "45, 34 | Parthian Empire" — CORRECTED: "Parthia, then Sasanian Persia (from 224)". Reason: coordinate (Ctesiphon/Babylonia) is right, but the Parthian Arsacids fall in 224 CE; the last century of the era faces the Sasanians. Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Palestine/Roman-Palestine (context) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Cyprus

## `byzantine` — Byzantine (330–1191 CE), power: Byzantine Empire

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "Origin: Eastern Roman Empire, ruled from Constantinople" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Cyprus/Byzantine-Empire
- CLAIM: "Seat: Salamis-Constantia, then Nicosia after the Arab raids" — CONFIRMED with a date: Constantia (capital from c. 346) declined after 649; Nicosia (Leukosia) became the administrative centre in the 9th/10th century and was the capital of the theme created in 965. Suggest "then Nicosia (capital of the theme from 965)". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_(theme) ; https://leventismuseum.org.cy/nicosia-the-birth-and-growth-of-a-capital-byzantine-medieval-period/ ; https://www.britannica.com/place/Nicosia
- CLAIM: "Cyprus was a Christian island with an independent church from 431 CE" — CONFIRMED (Council of Ephesus, canon 8; Britannica: "first recognized by the third ecumenical Council of Ephesus (431)"). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Church-of-Cyprus ; https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum03.htm
- CLAIM: "Arab fleets raided from 649, and for three centuries the island was a strange condominium, taxed by both Damascus and Constantinople" — CORRECTED: "Arab fleets raided from 649, and from 688 to 965 the island was a strange shared possession, paying tax to both the Caliphate and Constantinople." Reason: (a) the shared regime dates from 688, so "almost three centuries" (277 years); (b) the Caliphate moved from Damascus to Baghdad in 750, so "Damascus" is wrong for most of the period; (c) Britannica keeps the word "condominium" ("a kind of condominium"), but recent work (Zavagno) stresses it was a looser, contested arrangement, so soften to "a kind of condominium". Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Cyprus/Byzantine-Empire ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "Nikephoros Phokas recovered it in 965 and the coast was slowly resettled around new inland capitals" — CORRECTED: "In 965 Nikephoros II Phokas's general Niketas Chalkoutzes recovered it, Cyprus became a Byzantine theme, and Nicosia inland became the capital." Reason: the emperor did not come in person; "new inland capitals" (plural) overstates — only Nicosia. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niketas_Chalkoutzes ; https://www.britannica.com/place/Cyprus/Byzantine-Empire ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_(theme)
- CLAIM: "365 CE: A great earthquake destroys Kourion and other cities" — CONFIRMED with a caveat: Soren dated the Kourion destruction to the 21 July 365 quake; re-examination of the coins favours c. 370, and the site shows several quakes 365–370. Suggest "365–370 CE: Earthquakes flatten Kourion; it is rebuilt as a Christian town." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/365_Crete_earthquake
- CLAIM: "431 CE: The Church of Cyprus is declared independent at the Council of Ephesus" — CONFIRMED (canon 8, petition of Rheginos of Constantia against Antioch). Source: https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum03.htm ; https://www.britannica.com/topic/Church-of-Cyprus
- CLAIM: "649 CE: Arab fleet under Muawiya sacks Salamis-Constantia" — CONFIRMED (spring 649, c. 1,700 ships; second raid 650, garrison later). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Cyprus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_al-A%27war
- CLAIM: "688 CE: Treaty makes Cyprus a neutral condominium taxed by Arabs and Byzantines" — CONFIRMED (Justinian II and Abd al-Malik; revenue shared, island demilitarised; "a kind of condominium" per Britannica). Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Cyprus/Byzantine-Empire ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_II
- CLAIM: "965 CE: Nikephoros II Phokas recovers the island for Byzantium" — CONFIRMED (via Niketas Chalkoutzes; theme of Cyprus created). Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Cyprus/Byzantine-Empire ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niketas_Chalkoutzes
- CLAIM: "1185 CE: Isaac Komnenos seizes Cyprus and rules as a tyrant" — CONFIRMED (seized power late 1184 / c. 1185, called himself emperor; deposed by Richard I in May 1191). Suggest "1184/85". Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/Cyprus/Byzantine-Empire ; https://www.academia.edu/124663487/The_Tyrant_of_Cyprus_Isaac_Doukas_Komnenos_1184_1191_and_Margaritone_di_Brindisi_Ai%C3%B4nos_Miscellanea_di_Studi_Storici_26_2023_201_228
- CLAIM (from task list): "Zeno and the relics of Barnabas 488" — UNCERTAIN on the year: the Church of Cyprus and most Church histories give 478 for Anthemios's discovery; Britannica and Wikipedia give 488 for the discovery/Zeno's confirmation. Use "c. 478–488" or "480s". The three regalia privileges (purple ink, purple cloak, sceptre) are tradition: Huffman (JEH, Cambridge) argues they were first claimed in the late 16th century, so present them as "by tradition".
