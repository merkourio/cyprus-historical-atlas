# Report D

Scope: six southern/central sites in `slice_D_sites_south_centre.md`. Every dated or factual claim was checked against Department of Antiquities / Visit Cyprus pages, museum catalogues (Louvre, British Museum, Met, BnF), excavation-project pages (PULP, EFA, Kyprios Character, Liverpool World Museum), university news releases and peer-reviewed abstracts; Wikipedia used as a pointer and cross-checked where possible. The web-search budget ran out near the end, so a few secondary points (flagged UNCERTAIN) rest on single sources.

Totals: 62 claims checked — 44 CONFIRMED, 13 CORRECTED, 5 UNCERTAIN. 18 finds added (3 per site). 3 new sites proposed, plus 1 alternate.

## Site by site

### `palaepaphos` — Palaepaphos (Kouklia)

#### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "lon 32.58, lat 34.71" — CONFIRMED (Kouklia 34°42′26″N 32°34′24″E = 34.707, 32.573). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouklia
- CLAIM: "type kingdom" — CONFIRMED (Iron Age royal seat, identified epigraphically). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/archaeology/history-of-research-at-palaepaphos
- CLAIM: "Active 1600 BCE–400 CE" — CONFIRMED with nuance: earliest occupation is c. 1650 BCE (Late Cypriot I), so 1650 BCE would be slightly more accurate; the sanctuary went out of use in the late 4th century CE (earthquake damage; pagan cult banned 391). Sources: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/archaeology/history-of-research-at-palaepaphos ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouklia
- CLAIM: "Old Paphos was a Bronze Age town that became the Sanctuary of Aphrodite" — CONFIRMED (Late Bronze Age town; sanctuary built c. 1200 BCE). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/archaeology/history-of-research-at-palaepaphos
- CLAIM: "last king, Nikokles, founded Nea Paphos before Ptolemy ended the dynasty" — CONFIRMED. Nikokles moved his seat to Nea Paphos in the late 4th century BCE; Ptolemy I forced his suicide in 310/309 BCE. (A minority credits Ptolemy with the foundation.) Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicocles_of_Paphos ; https://www.attalus.org/names/n/nicocles.html ; https://ejournals.eu/en/journal_article_files/full_text/01987f49-16cb-724d-846f-0ca34341a9cb/download
- CLAIM: "Sanctuary of Aphrodite (c. 1200 BCE to 4th century CE)" — CONFIRMED (Maier's Swiss-German mission fixed construction at c. 1200 BCE). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/archaeology/history-of-research-at-palaepaphos
- CLAIM: "open-air sanctuary of megalithic walls and later Roman courtyards" — CONFIRMED (Sanctuary I: Late Bronze Age temenos with monumental limestone wall; Sanctuary II: Roman courtyards). Source: https://www.cyprushighlights.com/en/palaepaphos/
- CLAIM: "worshipped as a conical black stone rather than a statue" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.cyprushighlights.com/en/palaepaphos/
- CLAIM: "the baetyl in Kouklia Local Museum" — CONFIRMED (displayed in the Lusignan manor museum). Source: https://www.cyprushighlights.com/en/palaepaphos/
- CLAIM: "Persian siege mound (498 BCE)" — CORRECTED: "498/497 BCE" (the revolt began 499/498; the siege belongs to the Persian reconquest of 498/497). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouklia ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/paphos/paphos-photos/old-paphos-marchellos/
- CLAIM: "A ramp built by Persian troops against the north-east gate" — CONFIRMED (Marchello, "Northeast Gate" of the Swiss-German mission). Source: https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/collections/world-museum/site-kouklia-sculpture-collection
- CLAIM: "packed with hundreds of Archaic votive statues taken from a sanctuary" — CORRECTED: "packed with about a thousand fragments of Archaic statues, altars and inscribed stones, apparently torn from a nearby shrine or from buildings inside the city". About 1,000 sculptural pieces were recovered; whether they came from an extramural sanctuary or from buildings inside the walls is debated. Sources: https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/collections/world-museum/site-kouklia-sculpture-collection ; https://journals.openedition.org/cchyp/537?lang=en
- CLAIM: "The best-dated Persian siege works anywhere" — CORRECTED: "One of the earliest siege ramps ever excavated, and the only one that can be tied to Herodotus' account of the Persian reconquest of Cyprus." No source supports "best-dated anywhere"; recent PULP work even questions the siege-mound reading. Sources: https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/collections/world-museum/site-kouklia-sculpture-collection ; https://journals.openedition.org/cchyp/537?lang=en
- CLAIM: "Now: Sculptures in Cyprus Museum; ramp in situ" — CORRECTED: "Sculptures divided between the Kouklia Museum, the Cyprus Museum (Nicosia) and World Museum Liverpool; ramp in situ". Source: https://www.liverpoolmuseums.org.uk/collections/world-museum/site-kouklia-sculpture-collection
- CLAIM: "Cypro-syllabic inscriptions (7th–4th century BCE): The largest group of texts in the Cypriot syllabary" — CONFIRMED (the Marchello deposit alone yielded hundreds of inscribed stones, "the greatest single collection of syllabic inscriptions"). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/archaeology/history-of-research-at-palaepaphos
- CLAIM: "Now: Kouklia Local Museum; Cyprus Museum" — CONFIRMED; add "World Museum Liverpool". Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/archaeology/history-of-research-at-palaepaphos
- CLAIM: "Lusignan sugar refinery (14th–16th century)" — CORRECTED: "Late 13th–16th century". The Stavros refinery was built at the end of the 13th century and used until c. 1571; the manor is 13th century. Sources: https://www.paphoslife.com/blog/sugar_factory ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquaries-journal/article/abs/archaeology-of-cane-sugar-production-a-survey-of-twenty-years-of-research-in-cyprus/53836810C447B2E96060B060D1A16475
- CLAIM: "A royal manor and sugar mill" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.paphoslife.com/blog/sugar_factory

### `neapaphos` — Nea Paphos (Paphos)

#### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "lon 32.41, lat 34.755" — CONFIRMED (Kato Paphos harbour c. 34.755N, 32.41E). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos_Archaeological_Park
- CLAIM: "Active 320 BCE–2026 CE" — CONFIRMED (founded late 4th century BCE; continuously inhabited). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos_Archaeological_Park
- CLAIM: "Founded around 320 BCE as a harbour city" — CONFIRMED (late 4th century BCE, by Nikokles). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos_Archaeological_Park
- CLAIM: "became the capital of Ptolemaic and then Roman Cyprus" — CONFIRMED; more precisely it replaced Salamis as capital in the 2nd century BCE and remained capital until the 4th century CE. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos_Archaeological_Park
- CLAIM: "finest mosaics on the island" (UNESCO context) — CONFIRMED (World Heritage since 1980). Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/pafos-paphos-mosaics/
- CLAIM: "House of Dionysos mosaics (Late 2nd century CE)" — CONFIRMED (Visit Cyprus/DoA: late 2nd century CE; some references say late 2nd–early 3rd). Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/pafos-paphos-mosaics/
- CLAIM: "Dionysos, the seasons, Pyramos and Thisbe, Narcissus and the first wine" — CONFIRMED (Ikarios and the first wine-drinkers, Pyramos and Thisbe, Narcissus, Four Seasons). Source: https://www.thearchaeologist.org/blog/roman-mosaics-in-cyprus-the-house-of-dionysos-in-paphos
- CLAIM: "Found by a farmer ploughing in 1962" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/pafos-paphos-mosaics/
- CLAIM: "Villa of Theseus (2nd–5th century CE): residence of the Roman governor" — CONFIRMED (largest building known on Cyprus, probably the proconsul's palace; Polish mission since 1965). Source: https://www.gov.pl/web/cyprus/55-years-of-polish-archaeological-activity-1965-2020-of-the-university-of-warsaw-and-krakow-jagiellonian-university-in-paphos-unesco-world-heritage-site
- CLAIM: "round mosaic of Theseus and the Minotaur" — CONFIRMED (3rd century CE, circular labyrinth). Source: https://imperiumromanum.pl/en/article/polish-excavations-in-nea-paphos/
- CLAIM: "Tombs of the Kings (3rd century BCE onwards)" — CORRECTED: "4th century BCE to 3rd century CE". Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/tombs-of-the-kings/
- CLAIM: "Rock-cut peristyle tombs of Ptolemaic officials, imitating houses with Doric columns. No kings were ever buried here." — CONFIRMED (high officials and aristocrats; Alexandrian parallels). Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/tombs-of-the-kings/
- CLAIM: "Saranta Kolones (c. 1200 CE): A Lusignan castle on Byzantine foundations" — CORRECTED: "(late 7th century / c. 1200 CE): A castle first raised by the Byzantines to guard the harbour against Arab raids and remodelled by the Lusignans around 1200 (its excavator Megaw thought the whole fortress Frankish)". Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/saranta-kolones-forty-columns-castle/
- CLAIM: "wrecked by an earthquake in 1222 and never rebuilt" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/saranta-kolones-forty-columns-castle/

### `kourion` — Kourion (Episkopi)

#### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "lon 32.89, lat 34.665" — CONFIRMED (c. 34.664N, 32.888E). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion
- CLAIM: "Active 1200 BCE–650 CE" — CONFIRMED for the city; note the neighbouring Late Bronze Age town at Episkopi-Bamboula flourished in the 13th century BCE, and the acropolis was destroyed in the Arab raids of 648–649 CE. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion
- CLAIM: "by tradition founded by Argives" — CONFIRMED (Herodotus 5.113; Strabo). Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/rural/sites-monuments/kourion-archaeological-site-2/
- CLAIM: "Its king Stasanor deserted Onesilos at the battle of 498 BCE" — CORRECTED: "Its king Stesenor deserted Onesilos in the battle of 497 BCE". Herodotus names him Stesenor (Stasanor is a variant spelling); the battle is dated 497 BCE (revolt began 499/498). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onesilus ; https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/battles_salamis_497.html
- CLAIM: "until the earthquake of 365 CE" — CONFIRMED as the conventional date (21 July 365; Soren's coin evidence). Several quakes struck between 365 and c. 370, so "the earthquakes of 365–370" is the cautious form. Sources: https://news.arizona.edu/news/u-experts-discovery-be-displayed-cyprus-archaeological-museum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion
- CLAIM: "after which a Christian city rose and then faded away" — CONFIRMED (rebuilt early 5th century; abandoned after 648–649 for Episkopi). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion
- CLAIM: "The Earthquake House (365 CE)" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://news.arizona.edu/news/u-experts-discovery-be-displayed-cyprus-archaeological-museum
- CLAIM: "a man shielding a woman and child" — CONFIRMED: a man of about 25, a woman of about 19 and a child of about 18 months; a girl of about 13 also died, fallen from the upper floor. Sources: https://news.arizona.edu/news/u-experts-discovery-be-displayed-cyprus-archaeological-museum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion
- CLAIM: "a mule tethered in the yard" — CONFIRMED (chained to a stone trough in the courtyard/stable). Source: https://www.ringofchrist.com/early-christian-history/chi-rho/the-young-family/
- CLAIM: "The earthquake destruction layer dates the whole city" — UNCERTAIN: it dates the destruction of the Roman city, not earlier or later phases; suggest "dates the destruction of the Roman city". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourion
- CLAIM: "skeletons studied at Kourion Museum, Episkopi" — CONFIRMED with update: excavated by David Soren (University of Arizona) 1984–87, the house itself in 1986; the remains have been shown in the Kourion Museum at Episkopi and are to move to the new Cyprus Museum in Nicosia (opening 2027). Suggested wording: "In situ; remains in Kourion Museum, Episkopi (to be moved to the new Cyprus Museum, Nicosia)". Source: https://news.arizona.edu/news/u-experts-discovery-be-displayed-cyprus-archaeological-museum
- CLAIM: "Theatre (2nd century BCE, rebuilt 2nd century CE)" — CONFIRMED (late 2nd century BCE; enlarged under Trajan to c. 3,500 seats). Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/rural/sites-monuments/kourion-archaeological-site-2/
- CLAIM: "converted for animal fights" — CONFIRMED (altered c. 214–217 CE for gladiatorial and hunting shows). Source: https://www.worldhistory.org/image/10217/roman-theatre-in-kourion-cyprus/
- CLAIM: "restored for concerts today" — CONFIRMED (restored 1961). Source: https://www.worldhistory.org/image/10217/roman-theatre-in-kourion-cyprus/
- CLAIM: "House of Eustolios mosaics (Early 5th century CE)" — CONFIRMED (late 4th-century house, baths added early 5th century). Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/kourion-mosaics/
- CLAIM: "inscriptions praising Christ" — CONFIRMED ("this house is girt by the much-venerated signs of Christ"). Source: https://www.academia.edu/110250030/The_Ktisis_of_Early_Christian_Kourion_The_Cosmic_Symbolism_of_the_Mosaics_in_the_House_of_Eustolios_and_the_Emergence_of_Christian_Kourion
- CLAIM: "Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates (8th century BCE to 4th century CE)" — CONFIRMED (worship from the 8th century BCE to the 4th century CE; the standing temple is a 1st-century CE rebuild). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Hylates
- CLAIM: "a temple, a palaestra and a great pit of broken votives" — CONFIRMED (votive pit with terracottas from the 5th century BCE on). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Hylates
- CLAIM: "west of Kourion" — CONFIRMED (c. 2.5 km west). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Hylates
- CLAIM: "The Kourion Treasure … removed by Luigi Palma di Cesnola in the 1870s, with disputed provenance. Now: Metropolitan Museum of Art" — CONFIRMED (claimed find 1874–75; Met bought the collection). Sources: https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/ancient-art-from-cyprus-the-cesnola-collection-in-the-metropolitan-museum-of-art ; https://www.ancientcyprus.com/articles/iron-age-cities-kourion

### `idalion` — Idalion (Dali)

#### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "lon 33.42, lat 35.01" — CONFIRMED (35°00′57″N 33°25′23″E). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idalion
- CLAIM: "Active 1200 BCE–300 CE" — UNCERTAIN: the city on its present site is Late Bronze Age (c. 1200 BCE) with earlier settlement nearby; occupation continued through the Roman period, so 300 CE is defensible, but the west acropolis and palace were abandoned c. 300 BCE. Keep the range; note the Roman phase. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idalion
- CLAIM: "A city-kingdom on two acropolis hills near the copper mines, with temples to Athena and Aphrodite" — CONFIRMED (Athena on the west hill, Ambelleri; Aphrodite and Apollo on the east hill). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idalion
- CLAIM: "Around 470 BCE it was conquered by the Phoenician kings of Kition" — UNCERTAIN: sources range from c. 478–470 to c. 450 BCE (Wikipedia gives c. 450; the Idalion Tablet implies a siege c. 480–470). Suggest "in the mid-fifth century BCE (c. 470–450)". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idalion ; https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/inscriptions/tablet-of-idalion-ics-217
- CLAIM: "administered from a palace archive" — CONFIRMED (Phoenician administrative centre in the palace on the west acropolis). Source: https://www.archaeology.wiki/blog/2014/05/27/the-phoenician-archive-of-idalion/
- CLAIM: "one of the first ever excavated on Cyprus" — CONFIRMED (Cesnola 1867–75, Lang 1868–69). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idalion
- CLAIM: "The Idalion Tablet (c. 478–470 BCE)" — CONFIRMED (dated 480–470 BCE). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/inscriptions/tablet-of-idalion-ics-217
- CLAIM: "bronze plaque in the Cypriot syllabary recording the city's contract with a physician who treated the wounded during the siege by Persians and Kitians" — CONFIRMED (King Stasikypros and the city with the physician Onasilos and his brothers). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/inscriptions/tablet-of-idalion-ics-217
- CLAIM: "The longest syllabic text known" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/inscriptions/tablet-of-idalion-ics-217
- CLAIM: "Now: Bibliothèque nationale de France, Paris" — CONFIRMED (Cabinet des Médailles, Bronze.2297; found c. 1850, given by the duc de Luynes in 1862). Source: https://medaillesetantiques.bnf.fr/ws/catalogue/app/collection/record/ark:/12148/c33gbdzkg

### `tamassos` — Tamassos (Politiko)

#### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "lon 33.25, lat 35.02" — CONFIRMED (35.035N, 33.250E). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamassos
- CLAIM: "Active 1100 BCE–300 CE" — CORRECTED end date: "1100 BCE–700 CE". Tamassos was a bishopric from the 4th century (its first bishop Herakleidios has a basilica and monastery on the edge of the site) and only declined in the Byzantine period; the start date is plausible (Cypro-Geometric town; area settled from the Early Bronze Age). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamassos ; https://www.cyprushighlights.com/en/tamassos/
- CLAIM: "Homer calls it Temese, source of the bronze that armed the heroes" — CORRECTED: "Homer's Temese, where Athena in disguise sails to trade iron 'for copper', is usually identified with Tamassos, though some scholars place it in southern Italy". Homer speaks of a copper trade, not of arming heroes. Sources: https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/tamassus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamassos
- CLAIM: "wealth came from the mines of the north-east Troodos" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamassos
- CLAIM: "Royal tombs (6th century BCE): Two ashlar-built chamber tombs" — CONFIRMED (mid-6th century BCE; found by Max Ohnefalsch-Richter in 1889). Source: https://www.cyprushighlights.com/en/tamassos/
- CLAIM: "carved pilasters and false windows imitating a wooden house or a Phoenician shrine" — CONFIRMED (carved beams, door bolts and windows imitate timber construction; Phoenician and Egyptian influence). Source: https://cyprusdiscovery.com/ancient-tamassos-cyprus-copper-city-royal-tombs/
- CLAIM: "The Chatsworth Head (c. 460 BCE)" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatsworth_Head
- CLAIM: "life-size bronze head of Apollo from a statue found by villagers in 1836; the body was broken up for scrap" — CONFIRMED (found 1836 near Tamassos; the statue fell apart as villagers hauled it off and the limbs and torso were lost — a leg in the Louvre may belong to it; head 31.6 cm high). - CLAIM: "Now: British Museum, London" — CONFIRMED (bought by the 6th Duke of Devonshire 1838; acquired by the BM from Chatsworth in 1958, GR 1958.4-18.1). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatsworth_Head
- CLAIM: "Copper slag heaps (Bronze Age to Roman): Vast heaps … three thousand years of extraction" — UNCERTAIN: mining around Tamassos from the Bronze Age into Roman times is well attested, but "three thousand years" and "vast heaps" are not specifically sourced; suggest "heaps of smelting waste around the mines record extraction from the Bronze Age to Roman times". - CLAIM: "Sanctuary of Aphrodite-Astarte (Archaic to Roman)" — CORRECTED: "(6th century BCE to Hellenistic)".
