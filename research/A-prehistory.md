## ERA `aceramic`

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "Aceramic Neolithic (10000 BCE to 5500 BCE)" — CORRECTED: "Epipalaeolithic and Aceramic Neolithic (c. 10000 BCE to 5500 BCE)". Aetokremnos (c. 10,000 BCE) is an Epipalaeolithic hunters' camp; the Aceramic Neolithic proper begins c. 9000/8800 BCE with Klimonas. End c. 5500–5200 BCE is fine. Source: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/compendium/chronology ; https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1201693109
- CLAIM: "Origin: Farmers arriving by sea from the Levant and Anatolia" — CONFIRMED (PPNA-type cultivators from the northern Levant; obsidian shows Anatolian links). Source: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1201693109
- CLAIM: "Cyprus was settled around 9000 BCE by people who brought domesticated wheat, barley, sheep, goats, pigs and cattle across open sea" — CORRECTED: "Cyprus was settled around 8800 BCE by cultivators who shipped in wheat, barley and dogs; wild boar had already been introduced by earlier hunters, and cattle, sheep and goats followed by boat over the next few centuries." Klimonas (c. 8800 BCE) had cereals and dogs but hunted boar; cattle c. 8500 BCE and sheep c. 7900 BCE arrive at Shillourokambos. Source: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1201693109 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26766529_Pre-Neolithic_wild_boar_management_and_introduction_to_Cyprus_more_than_11400_years_ago ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277379126001356
- CLAIM: "round stone houses in tight villages" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirokitia
- CLAIM: "made bowls from grey andesite rather than clay" — CONFIRMED (andesite and diabase vessels). Source: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/sites/khirokitia
- CLAIM: "There was no ruler above the village" — CONFIRMED (no evidence of central authority; standard interpretation).
- CLAIM: "10500 BCE: Hunters camp at Akrotiri-Aetokremnos, the earliest trace of people on Cyprus" — CONFIRMED, with a nuance: c. 12,000 cal BP, i.e. c. 10,000 BCE (range c. 10,500–9,500 BCE). Shillourokambos begins c. 8400/8200 BCE and the cat burial is c. 7500 BCE (9,500 years old). Source: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2004/04/040409092827.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shillourokambos ; https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1201693109

- CLAIM: "There is no pottery yet anywhere in the Near East when the first Cypriots arrive" — CONFIRMED (Near Eastern pottery begins c. 7000 BCE; Cypriots present by 8800 BCE). Source: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1201693109

## ERA `ceramic`

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "Ceramic Neolithic (5500 BCE to 3900 BCE)" — CONFIRMED as a bracket (the era covers the gap plus the Sotira culture, c. 4500–3900/3800 BCE).
- CLAIM: "Origin: Indigenous, after a gap of about a thousand years" — UNCERTAIN: sources give the gap as anything from a few centuries (Knapp: Ceramic Neolithic from 5200/5000) to c. 1,000 years (academia.edu paper) to 1,500 years (Wikipedia); a minority argue there was no real gap. Suggested wording: "Indigenous, after a gap of several centuries whose length is still debated". Source: https://www.academia.edu/13308394/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "new farming communities appeared around 4800 BCE" and event "4800 BCE: New villages appear" — CORRECTED: "around 4500 BCE" (Sotira culture c. 4500/4400–3900/3800 BCE). Source: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/compendium/chronology ; https://www.ancientcyprus.com/sites/sotira
- CLAIM: "They made the first Cypriot pottery, painted and combed" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://www.penn.museum/sites/bulletin/3596/
- CLAIM: "4500 BCE: Sotira on the south coast and Ayios Epiktitos-Vrysi in the north flourish" — CONFIRMED (Sotira c. 4500–3800; Vrysi c. 4400–3800). Source: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/sites/sotira ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghios_Epiktitos_Vrysi
- CLAIM: "4000 BCE: Combed Ware pottery reaches its peak" — UNCERTAIN: Combed Ware is the hallmark of the southern Sotira culture, but no source gives a "peak" date. Suggest "Combed Ware is the signature pottery of the southern villages".
- CLAIM: "3900 BCE: The Sotira culture ends, perhaps after earthquakes" — CONFIRMED with a nuance: the earthquake is attested at Sotira-Teppes itself (most house superstructures collapsed); the culture ends c. 3900/3800 BCE. Source: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/sites/sotira ; https://www.penn.museum/sites/bulletin/3596/
- CLAIM: "Ubaid culture unites Mesopotamia" — CONFIRMED (Ubaid c. 6500/5200–3800 BCE). Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ubaid-Period
- CLAIM: "Predynastic Egypt begins along the Nile" — CONFIRMED (Badarian c. 4400–4000 BCE). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badarian_culture
- CLAIM: "Copper metallurgy is emerging in Anatolia and the Balkans" — UNCERTAIN: broadly correct (Balkan smelting c. 5000 BCE) but not re-verified this session.
- CLAIM: label "40.5, 36.6: Halaf culture" — CORRECTED: Halaf runs c. 6100–5100 BCE, so it overlaps only the first centuries of this era; either keep with the note "(ends c. 5100 BCE)" or relabel "Halaf, then Ubaid, in north Syria". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halaf_culture
- CLAIM: labels "45.5, 32 Ubaid", "31.3, 27 Badarian", "33.5, 38.8 Anatolian villages" — CONFIRMED as approximate placements.

## SITE `aetokremnos`

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "lon 32.97, lat 34.57" — CONFIRMED (site at 32.99 E, 34.57 N; c. 2 km). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetokremnos
- CLAIM: "Active 10500 BCE–9500 BCE" — CONFIRMED (c. 12,500–11,500 cal BP; mean c. 11,775 BP). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aetokremnos ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270573019_The_Dating_of_Akrotiri_Aetokremnos
