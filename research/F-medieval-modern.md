# Report F

Method: every dated claim in slice F was checked against at least one web source (Wikipedia used as pointer; confirmed where possible with Britannica summaries, INA / PLOS One for the Kyrenia Ship, UNESCO, Library of Congress Country Studies, Cambridge "History of Cyprus" chapter titles, academia.edu abstracts, visitcyprus.com). Britannica and UNESCO pages returned 403 to direct fetch, so they are cited via search-result summaries where used. Web search budget ran out after the site batch; the remaining checks were done by fetching pages directly.

Legend: CONFIRMED = keep as is; CORRECTED = replace with the quoted wording; UNCERTAIN = keep but soften or flag.

## Site `kyrenia`

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "A small ancient kingdom that grew into a walled Byzantine harbour town" — UNCERTAIN: Kyrenia (Keryneia) is named among the city-kingdoms in the 4th century BCE, but the 1000 BCE start is not documented for Kyrenia itself; keep the range but treat the first centuries as inferred. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_Castle
- CLAIM: "the best-preserved Greek merchant vessel ever found" — CORRECTED: "one of the best-preserved ancient Greek merchant ships ever found" (Kyrenia is the type-site, but Marsala, Ma'agan Michael and others also survive well). Source: https://nauticalarch.org/projects/kyrenia-shipwreck-excavation/
- CLAIM: Kyrenia Ship "Sank c. 295 BCE" — CORRECTED: "Sank c. 290–280 BCE" (final report 2022: 294–290 BCE from coins and pottery; 2024 radiocarbon study: 95% range 315–270 BCE, most likely the 280s). Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0302645 ; https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv13pk6bp
- CLAIM: "A 14-metre merchant ship" — CONFIRMED (about 14 m long, 4.4 m beam). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_ship ; INA
- CLAIM: "built around 315 BCE" — UNCERTAIN: c. 315 BCE is the traditional estimate; the 2024 study only shows the hull timbers date to the mid-4th century BCE or later and that the ship was old and much repaired when she sank. Suggest "built in the later 4th century BCE and already old when she sank". Source: PLOS One above
- CLAIM: "found by a sponge diver in 1965" — CONFIRMED with nuance (Andreas Cariolou, a Kyrenia diving instructor and sponge diver, November 1965; relocated 1967; excavated 1967–69 by Michael Katzev of the University of Pennsylvania). Suggest naming him. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_ship ; https://nauticalarch.org/projects/kyrenia-shipwreck-excavation/
- CLAIM: "400 Rhodian wine amphorae" — CORRECTED: "about 380 wine amphorae, 340 of them from Rhodes" (INA's early estimate was "as many as 500"). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_Shipwreck_Museum
- CLAIM: "10,000 almonds" — CORRECTED: "more than 9,000 almonds, and 29 millstones carried as ballast". Source: same
- CLAIM: "Her hull is three-quarters intact" — CORRECTED: "More than half of her hull timbers survived" (75% is often quoted but the excavators say "more than half"). Source: same
- CLAIM: "Now: Shipwreck Museum, Kyrenia Castle" — CONFIRMED.
- CLAIM: Kyrenia Castle "7th century, rebuilt 1208–1211 and 1540s" — CONFIRMED; tighten to "1540" (Byzantine fort against Arab raids; enlarged by John of Ibelin 1208–11; besieged by Genoa 1373; rebuilt with round towers by Venice in 1540; surrendered to the Ottomans in 1570 without a fight). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrenia_Castle
- CLAIM: Bellapais Abbey "13th–14th century ... Premonstratensian" — CONFIRMED with nuance: founded 1198–1205 by King Aimery for Augustinian canons who had fled Jerusalem; Premonstratensian from 1206; main church under Hugh III (1267–84); cloister and refectory under Hugh IV (1324–59); sacked by Genoa 1373. Suggest period "c.1200–14th century". "Finest Gothic monastery in the Levant" is a fair but subjective phrase; prefer "often called the finest". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellapais_Abbey

## Site `nicosia`

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "A minor Iron Age kingdom called Ledra" — CONFIRMED (Ledra is listed on Esarhaddon's prism c. 673 BCE with King Onasagoras; a village by Hellenistic times; bishopric from the 4th century; capital from about the 10th century). The active-range gap 300 BCE–300 CE is defensible but the site was continuously (if thinly) occupied; consider a single range 1000 BCE–2026 CE. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledra

- CLAIM: "Venice ringed it with eleven bastions" — CONFIRMED.
- CLAIM: Venetian walls "1567–1570 ... perfect circle ... eleven heart-shaped bastions ... three miles round and still complete" — CORRECTED: "A near-circle of ramparts about 5 km (3 miles) round, with eleven heart-shaped bastions designed by Giulio Savorgnan and Francesco Barbaro and three gates; still unfinished when the Ottomans stormed the Podocattaro bastion on 9 September 1570." ("Still complete" is fine for the circuit today.) Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Nicosia ; https://files.acquia.undp.org/public/migration/cy/UNDP_CY_FS_QuiriniBastion.pdf
- CLAIM: Cathedral of Saint Sophia "1209–1326 ... coronation church ... French Gothic ... mosque since 1571 (Selimiye)" — CORRECTED on one point: "a mosque since 1570" (converted within days of the city's fall, 9–15 September 1570; renamed Selimiye only in 1954). ### Verdicts

## Site `limassol`

### Verdicts
- CLAIM: "Active ranges 1000 CE–2026 CE" and "Nemesos grew up between the dead cities of Amathus and Kourion after the Arab raids" — CORRECTED: a bishop of Neapolis/Theodosias (Limassol) attended the Council of Chalcedon in 451, and the site had an Early Christian basilica (4th–7th century), so suggest active range "400 CE–2026 CE" and "Neapolis, later Nemesos/Lemesos, grew up between Amathus and Kourion and took over as they declined; Richard destroyed Amathus in 1191 and moved its people here." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limassol ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limassol_Castle
- CLAIM: "Richard the Lionheart landed here in 1191 and married Berengaria of Navarre in its castle" — CORRECTED: "married Berengaria of Navarre here on 12 May 1191, by tradition in the chapel of St George on the site of the later castle" (the castle post-dates the wedding; tradition only). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limassol_Castle
- CLAIM: "The Knights Templar and Hospitallers made the surrounding plain their estate" — CONFIRMED (Kolossi granted to the Hospitallers c. 1210; Templar interlude 1306–13; the Grand Commandery from 1291). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolossi_Castle
- CLAIM: "Commandaria, the world's oldest named wine" — CONFIRMED as the standard claim ("one of the oldest named wines in the world, having had the same name for eight centuries" per Visit Cyprus); safer wording "often called the world's oldest named wine still made". Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/kolossi-medieval-castle/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandaria
- CLAIM: Limassol Castle "Lusignan, rebuilt under the Ottomans ... now the Cyprus Medieval Museum" — CONFIRMED (built c. 1193 by Guy de Lusignan on a Byzantine site, first recorded 1228; damaged by Genoese and Mamluks; demolished by Venice 1567–68; Ottoman fort completed c. 1590; prison until 1950; Cyprus Medieval Museum since 1987). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limassol_Castle ; https://allaboutlimassol.com/en/cyprus-medieval-museum-limassol-castle
- CLAIM: Kolossi Castle "1454 ... Hospitaller commandery ... three-storey keep beside the sugar factory" — CONFIRMED (present keep built 1454 by Commander Louis de Magnac, 21 m high, three storeys; sugar production a main export). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolossi_Castle ; https://www.historyhit.com/locations/kolossi-castle/
- CLAIM: Medieval sugar mill "14th–16th century ... beside Kolossi" — UNCERTAIN on dates: Visit Cyprus calls it a "14th century sugar mill"; production continued into the Ottoman 16th century. Wording fine; keep. Source: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/kolossi-medieval-castle/
