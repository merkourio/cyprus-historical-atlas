## Era `lba` — Middle and Late Bronze Age (1900–1050 BCE)

### Verdicts

- CLAIM: "Power shown: Alashiya" — CONFIRMED for the Late Bronze Age (c. 1600–1050). UNCERTAIN for the Middle Bronze Age half of the era: the name Alashiya is not attested before c. 1600 (first in Mari/Alalakh texts, c. 18th–17th c.; Hittite claim c. 1400). Suggest the era card say "Alashiya (from c. 1600 BCE)". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alashiya
- CLAIM: "Cypriot kingdom, later drawn into the Hittite and Egyptian orbit" — CORRECTED: "A Cypriot kingdom that dealt with Egypt as an equal and was forced into vassalage by the Hittites around 1200 BCE." Egypt never ruled Alashiya; the Amarna letters use 'brother' language between equals. Hittite kings Tudhaliya IV and Suppiluliuma II campaigned against Alashiya c. 1220–1200 BCE, took the king to Hattusa and imposed tribute. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alashiya ; http://scholar.uoa.gr/sites/default/files/kkopanias/files/paper_2022_fs_mantzrourani.pdf
- CLAIM: "Seat: Enkomi, then coastal towns like Kition and Hala Sultan Tekke" — UNCERTAIN. Enkomi is the traditional candidate for the capital, but petrographic analysis (2003) of the Alashiya letters points to clay from the south coast near Kalavasos and Alassa, and there is no proof of a single centralised authority. Suggested wording: "Seat: unknown; Enkomi is the usual guess, but the clay of the Alashiya letters points to the south coast (Kalavasos, Alassa). Other big towns: Kition, Hala Sultan Tekke." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alashiya
- CLAIM: "copper-exporting kingdom whose king wrote to the pharaoh as an equal in the Amarna letters" — CONFIRMED (EA 33–40; "Say to the king of Egypt, my brother: message of the king of Alashiya, your brother"; 500 talents of copper in EA 35). Sources: https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/ea35/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letter_EA_35
- CLAIM: "Towns had ashlar buildings, a script of their own, and warehouses full of oxhide copper ingots" — CONFIRMED. Ashlar administrative buildings at Enkomi, Kition, Hala Sultan Tekke (LC II); Cypro-Minoan script c. 1500–1100; oxhide ingots (Uluburun wreck c. 1320 carried 10 tonnes of Cypriot copper). Sources: https://www.oeaw.ac.at/en/oeai/research/prehistory-wana-archaeology/mediterranean-economies/excavations-at-the-late-bronze-age-harbour-city-of-hala-sultan-tekke ; https://www.britannica.com/topic/Cypro-Minoan-script ; https://nauticalarch.org/projects/uluburun-late-bronze-age-shipwreck-excavation/
- CLAIM: "Mycenaean Greeks arrived around 1200 BCE, first as traders, then as settlers" — CORRECTED: "Mycenaean pottery reached Cyprus in quantity from the 14th century BCE, probably carried by Cypriot and Levantine ships; Greek-speaking settlers arrived only after 1200 BCE." The 'traders' phase is 14th–13th c., not c. 1200, and there is little evidence of Mycenaeans themselves trading on the island. Source: https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/annual-of-the-british-school-at-athens/article/social-impact-of-mycenaean-imported-pottery-in-cyprus1/101B8455A3B5C2D74F1F491D350AB556
- CLAIM: "1650 BCE: Fortresses guard the routes from the copper mines to the coast" — CONFIRMED (Middle Cypriot III–Late Cypriot I forts such as Nitovikla, c. 1725–1450; interpreted as elite control of copper routes, though this reading is debated). Suggest "c. 1700 BCE". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitovikla
- CLAIM: "1450 BCE: Cypro-Minoan script in use; Enkomi becomes a metalworking city" — CONFIRMED (earliest tablet, Enkomi, c. 1525–1425; Enkomi copper workshops from LC I). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypro-Minoan_script ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/enkomi/
- CLAIM: "1350 BCE: The king of Alashiya writes to Akhenaten in the Amarna letters" — CONFIRMED (Amarna archive c. 1360–1330; letters addressed to Amenhotep III and/or Akhenaten; 'Akhenaten' is fine as the usual shorthand). Source: https://www.worldhistory.org/Amarna_Letters/
- CLAIM: "1200 BCE: Mycenaean Greeks settle at Maa-Palaeokastro and other sites" — CONFIRMED (fortified site founded c. 1200, LC IIIA; abandoned mid-12th c.). Note the settlers' ethnicity is inferred from pottery and is debated; "Aegean settlers" is the safer phrase. Sources: https://www.visitcyprus.com/discover-cyprus/culture/sites-and-monuments/maa-palaeokastro-archaeological-site-and-museum/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maa_Palaeokastro
- CLAIM: "1050 BCE: Earthquakes and upheaval: Enkomi is abandoned, Salamis founded nearby" — CONFIRMED (earthquake c. 1050; Salamis's earliest finds are 11th c., 2 km away). Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/enkomi/ ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/salamis-cyprus/
- CLAIM: "The Hittite Empire and New Kingdom Egypt compete for the Levant" — CONFIRMED (13th c.; Kadesh 1274). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites
- CLAIM: "Ugarit and the Mycenaean palaces trade with Cyprus" — CONFIRMED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugarit
- CLAIM: "Around 1200 BCE the Bronze Age world collapses" — CONFIRMED (Hattusa c. 1180, Ugarit c. 1190, Pylos c. 1180). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece
- LABEL: "33.5, 39.5: Hittite Empire" — position CONFIRMED (central Anatolia inside the Halys bend, west of Hattusa). ANACHRONISM: no Hittite state before c. 1650 and no "Empire" before c. 1430; gone by c. 1180. Suggest "Hittites (from c. 1650)" or accept if the regional map is understood as a Late Bronze snapshot. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites
- LABEL: "31, 27: New Kingdom Egypt" — position CONFIRMED (Middle Egypt, Nile valley near Asyut). ANACHRONISM: New Kingdom is 1550–1070; the era begins in the Middle Kingdom. Suggest "Egypt (New Kingdom from 1550)". Source: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/egypt-in-the-late-period-ca-712-332-b-c (for Egyptian period framework) 
- LABEL: "22.3, 37.6: Mycenaean Greece" — position CONFIRMED (Peloponnese, Arcadia near Tripoli; Mycenae itself is 22.75E 37.73N). ANACHRONISM: Mycenaean civilisation c. 1750/1600–1050; fine for the LBA half. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece
- LABEL: "35.8, 35.6: Ugarit" — CONFIRMED (Ras Shamra 35.78E 35.60N). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugarit
- LABEL: "42.5, 36.4: Mitanni, then Assyria" — position acceptable (northern Iraq between Sinjar and Mosul: Assyrian heartland; Mitanni's core was further west on the Khabur, c. 40.5E). Wording CONFIRMED (Mitanni c. 1500–1340, Middle Assyrian after). If you want Mitanni properly under the label, move to 41.0, 36.6. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alashiya (context)

## Era `iron` — Iron Age (1050–750 BCE)

### Verdicts

- CLAIM: "Origin: Greek settlers over an indigenous population, Phoenicians at Kition" — CONFIRMED (Eteocypriot survival at Amathus; Tyrian settlers at Kition from c. 850). Sources: https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/monde/en/amathus ; https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kition/
- CLAIM: "Seat: Independent kingdoms: Salamis, Paphos, Kourion, Amathus, Kition and others" — CONFIRMED, though kingdoms as such are first attested in 673–672 (Esarhaddon prism). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/cyprus-and-others/cyprus-and-near-east-in-neo-assyrian-period
- CLAIM: "a dozen small kingdoms" — CORRECTED: "about ten small kingdoms" (Esarhaddon lists ten in 673–672; later sources add Lapithos, Marion, Kyrenia at various dates). Source: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/saao/aebp/Essentials/Countries/Cyprus/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_city-kingdoms_of_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "buried their kings with horses and chariots" — UNCERTAIN as an Iron Age (pre-750) trait. The Salamis royal tombs with horse and chariot burials date from the late 8th to early 6th century, i.e. the very end of this era and into the Archaic. Suggest moving this sentence to the Archaic card or writing "would soon bury their kings...". Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/salamis-cyprus/ ; https://www.ancientcyprus.com/sites/salamis
- CLAIM: "shared the island with a Phoenician kingdom at Kition" — CORRECTED: "shared the island with a Phoenician colony at Kition." Phoenician settlement is c. 850–800; a Phoenician kingdom there (Qart-hadasht) is first attested in 673–672. Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kition/ ; https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/cyprus-and-others/cypriotes-and-phoenicians
- CLAIM: "No one power ruled the whole island" — CONFIRMED.
- CLAIM: "1050 BCE: New towns at Salamis, Paphos, Kourion and Lapithos" — CORRECTED: "New towns at Salamis and Lapithos; Palaepaphos and Kourion, older centres, grow into kingdoms." Palaepaphos (Kouklia) and Kourion were already Late Bronze Age sites; only Salamis (and Lapithos-Kastros cemetery) are new 11th-century foundations. Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/salamis-cyprus/ ; https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0006%3Aentry%3Dkourion
- CLAIM: "1000 BCE: Greek is spoken, written in the Cypriot syllabary" — CONFIRMED (Opheltas spit, Palaepaphos-Skales tomb 49, c. 1050–950 BCE, Greek name in the genitive in an early Cypriot syllabary). Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275595876_The_most_Ancient_Cypriot_text_Written_in_Greek_The_Opheltas'_Spit
- CLAIM: "850 BCE: Phoenicians from Tyre settle at Kition and build a temple to Astarte" — CONFIRMED (Kathari temple c. 850; earliest Phoenician inscription on a bowl dedicated to Astarte c. 800). Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kition/ ; https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/cyprus-and-others/cypriotes-and-phoenicians
- CLAIM: "750 BCE: The first royal tombs at Salamis, with horse burials and ivory furniture" — CORRECTED: "c. 750–700 BCE (late 8th century): the first royal tombs at Salamis..." The series runs late 8th to early 6th century; Tomb 79 (the ivory furniture and cauldron tomb) is end of the 8th century. Sources: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/sites/salamis ; http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0006%3Aentry%3Dsalamis-1
- CLAIM: "The Greek Dark Ages give way to Homer's world" — CONFIRMED (Homeric poems c. 750–700; Homer names Kinyras of Cyprus). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinyras
- CLAIM: "Phoenician cities expand across the Mediterranean" — CONFIRMED (Kition c. 850, Carthage trad. 814).
- CLAIM: "The Neo-Assyrian Empire begins its rise" — CONFIRMED (from Adad-nirari II, 911 BCE). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire
- LABEL: "35.4, 33.7: Phoenician cities" — wording CONFIRMED; position MARGINAL: the Lebanese shoreline at 33.7N is at about 35.43E, so this point sits on or just off the beach south of Beirut. Move to 35.6, 33.7 to be safely inland.
- LABEL: "43, 36: Neo-Assyrian Empire" — CONFIRMED (near Mosul/Nineveh). Fine for 911 onward; for 1050–911 the label is slightly early ("Assyria" alone would be safe).
- LABEL: "35.2, 31.8: Israel and Judah" — CONFIRMED (Jerusalem). Kingdoms split c. 930; "Israel and Judah" fits most of the era.
- LABEL: "23, 38.4: Greek Dark Age" — position CONFIRMED (Boeotia). Wording awkward as a place label; suggest "Greece (Dark Age)".
- LABEL: "31.5, 39.5: Phrygia" — CONFIRMED (near Gordion 32.0E 39.65N). Phrygia as a kingdom peaks in the 8th century under Midas; Phrygians present in Anatolia from c. 1200, so acceptable. Source: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/phrygia-gordion-and-king-midas-in-the-late-eighth-century-b-c

## Era `archaic` — Archaic (750–480 BCE)

### Verdicts

- CLAIM: "Seven Cypriot kings submitted to Sargon II of Assyria in 707 BCE" — CONFIRMED with a note: Sargon's annals put the submission of the "seven kings of Ia', a district of Iadnana" in his 13th year (709); the Kition stele is usually dated 707. Either year appears in good sources; "c. 709–707" is the safest. Sources: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/cyprus-and-others/cyprus-and-near-east-in-neo-assyrian-period ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargon_Stele
- CLAIM: "his stele is set up at Kition" — CONFIRMED (found at Larnaca 1845, now in Berlin; exact findspot in Kition uncertain). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargon_Stele
- CLAIM: "Egypt claimed the island under Amasis" — CONFIRMED (Herodotus 2.182: Amasis "first to conquer Cyprus and make it tributary"). Source: https://www.loebclassics.com/view/LCL120/1925/pb_LCL120.331.xml?readMode=recto
- CLAIM: "570 BCE: Amasis of Egypt brings the island under Egyptian influence" — CORRECTED: "c. 560 BCE: Amasis of Egypt (r. 570–526) makes Cyprus tributary to Egypt." 570 is simply Amasis's accession year; Herodotus gives no date, and reference works place the conquest c. 560. Sources: https://www.worldhistory.org/cyprus/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amasis_II
- CLAIM: "from 545 BCE the Persians took it into their empire" — UNCERTAIN. 545 (or 546) is the conventional inference from Cyrus's conquest of Lydia and the Levant; no source dates the event. What is attested is Herodotus 3.19: the Cypriots "had joined the Persians of their own accord" by Cambyses's Egyptian campaign of 525. Suggested wording: "c. 545 BCE: Cyprus passes to the Persian Empire of Cyrus the Great (certainly Persian by 525)". Sources: https://www.worldhistory.org/cyprus/ ; https://classics.mit.edu/Herodotus/history.3.iii.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history_of_Cyprus
- CLAIM: "the city-kingdoms kept their own dynasties, coinage and quarrels" — CONFIRMED (Evelthon of Salamis strikes the first Cypriot coins c. 560–525; kings paid tribute but kept mints). Source: https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/numismatics/coinages-of-kings-of-cyprus-from-archaic-to-early-hellenistic-period
- CLAIM: "600 BCE: Cypriot limestone sculpture and terracottas reach their height" — CONFIRMED in outline (Cypro-Archaic II, 600–480, is the peak of limestone sculpture; the Ayia Irini sanctuary yielded c. 2,000 terracottas of the 7th–6th c.). "Height" is a judgement, so keep "c." UNCERTAIN only on the precise year. Source: https://www.ancientcyprus.com/compendium/chronology/late-bronze-age (site chronology pages) 
- CLAIM: "498 BCE: Onesilos of Salamis leads a revolt against Persia and is defeated" — CONFIRMED (revolt joined 499; Onesilos killed in the battle near Salamis 498/497; Soli held out five months). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onesilus ; https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyprus-achaemenid/
- CLAIM: "Assyria conquers and then falls to Babylon" — CONFIRMED (Nineveh 612). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire
- CLAIM: "Saite Egypt revives" — CONFIRMED (664–525). Source: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/egypt-in-the-late-period-ca-712-332-b-c
- CLAIM: "The Achaemenid Persian Empire stretches from India to the Aegean" — CONFIRMED for c. 515–480 (Darius). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire
- CLAIM: "Greek cities colonise the Mediterranean" — CONFIRMED (c. 750–550).
- LABEL: "43, 36: Neo-Assyrian Empire" — position CONFIRMED. ANACHRONISM: Assyria falls in 612, only halfway through the era; Babylon (626–539) then Persia hold the region. Suggest "Assyria (to 612), then Babylon". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Babylonian_Empire
- LABEL: "31, 27.5: Saite Egypt" — position CONFIRMED (Nile valley, Middle Egypt). ANACHRONISM: Persian from 525. Suggest "Saite Egypt (Persian from 525)".
- LABEL: "28.5, 38.6: Lydia" — CONFIRMED (inland east of Sardis). Lydia is Persian after 546; acceptable for an Archaic map. Source: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Mermnad-dynasty
- LABEL: "35.5, 33.8: Phoenicia" — wording CONFIRMED; position MARGINAL (Lebanese shoreline at 33.8N is c. 35.47E, so the point is on the beach). Move to 35.6, 33.8.
- LABEL: "27, 37.9: Ionian Greeks" — position WRONG: this is open sea between Samos and the Kuşadası coast. Move to 27.6, 38.2 (inland between Ephesus and Smyrna).

## Era `classical` — Classical (480–323 BCE)

### Verdicts

- CLAIM: "Origin: Persian Empire, satrapy of Babylonia-and-Beyond-the-River" — UNCERTAIN/CORRECTED. Herodotus (3.91) puts Cyprus in the fifth tax district with Phoenicia and Syria-Palestine; the combined satrapy "Babylon and Beyond the River" had been split by the 480s. Suggested wording: "Persian Empire, grouped with Phoenicia and Syria in the province 'Beyond the River'". Source: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyprus-achaemenid/
- CLAIM: "Seat: Local kings ruled; Persian garrisons and Phoenician allies at Kition and elsewhere" — CONFIRMED in outline (Pausanias raided Persian garrisons in 478; the Idalion tablet records 'the Medes and the Kitians' besieging Idalion c. 470s; Kition was consistently pro-Persian). Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/person/pausanias/ ; https://kyprioscharacter.eie.gr/en/scientific-texts/details/inscriptions/tablet-of-idalion-ics-217
- CLAIM: "Cyprus was a Persian satrapy" — CORRECTED: "Cyprus lay within a Persian satrapy" (the island was never a satrapy in its own right; no satrap of Cyprus is known). Source: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyprus-achaemenid/
- CLAIM: "Salamis under Evagoras I became a centre of Greek culture and a thorn in the Persian side" — CONFIRMED (Isocrates, Evagoras; Greek alphabet on coins; war 391–c. 380). Source: https://www.cnp.ed.ac.uk/index.php?event=salamis
- CLAIM: "Athens twice sent fleets to pull the island into the Greek world" — CORRECTED: "Greek fleets came three times: Pausanias's Hellenic League fleet in 478, an Athenian fleet of 200 ships c. 460, and Kimon in 450." Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/person/pausanias/ ; https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Thucydides,_translated_into_English_Vol_1.djvu/186 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimon
- CLAIM: "Persian control was real but never total" — CONFIRMED.
- CLAIM: "480 BCE: Cypriot ships fight for Persia at Salamis in Greece" — CONFIRMED (150 ships, third largest contingent, Herodotus 7.90 and 7.98). Source: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cyprus-achaemenid/
- CLAIM: "450 BCE: The Athenian general Kimon dies besieging Kition" — CONFIRMED (450 or 449; sources split; Plutarch, Cimon 19; Thucydides 1.112). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimon ; https://lexundria.com/plut_cim/4/prr
- CLAIM: "411 BCE: Evagoras I takes Salamis and makes it a beacon of Greek culture" — CONFIRMED (expelled the Phoenician usurper Abdemon in 411). Sources: https://www.britannica.com/biography/Evagoras ; https://www.cnp.ed.ac.uk/index.php?event=salamis
- CLAIM: "381 BCE: Evagoras defeated by Persia after a ten-year war, but keeps his throne" — UNCERTAIN on the year; substance CONFIRMED. Isocrates says the war lasted ten years (391–c. 380). Modern works give 380/379 (Livius, Iranica) or 376 (Britannica, following Diodorus's misplaced chronology). Peace terms: Evagoras stays king of Salamis, pays tribute, obeys the Great King "as a king to a king" (Diodorus 15.9). Suggested wording: "c. 380 BCE: After a ten-year war Evagoras makes peace with Persia; he pays tribute but keeps his throne 'as a king to a king'." Sources: https://www.livius.org/articles/place/cyprus/cyprus-3/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evagoras_I ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Evagoras
- CLAIM: "333 BCE: After Issus the Cypriot kings side with Alexander" — CONFIRMED (Issus, November 333; the Cypriot kings brought 120 ships to Alexander at Tyre in 332). Suggest "333–332 BCE". Source: https://www.worldhistory.org/article/107/alexanders-siege-of-tyre-332-bce/
- CLAIM: "The Persian Wars and the Athenian Empire. Athens and Sparta fight the Peloponnesian War. Philip II and Alexander of Macedon conquer Persia." — CONFIRMED.
- LABEL: "44, 34: Achaemenid Persia" — position is central Iraq (Babylonia), which was Persian territory but not Persia. Either rename "Persian Empire (Babylonia)" or, if the map extends east, move to 52.5, 30 (Persepolis/Fars). Wording otherwise fine.
- LABEL: "23.7, 38: Athens" — CONFIRMED.
- LABEL: "31, 28: Egypt, a Persian satrapy" — ANACHRONISM (serious): Egypt was independent under its 28th–30th dynasties from 404 to 343, i.e. for 61 of the era's 157 years, and it bankrolled Evagoras. CORRECTED: "Egypt (Persian to 404, independent 404–343, Persian again 343–332)" or simply "Egypt". Source: https://www.britannica.com/place/ancient-Egypt/Egypt-under-Achaemenid-rule
- LABEL: "35.5, 33.8: Phoenicia" — position MARGINAL (on the shoreline); move to 35.6, 33.8.
- LABEL: "22.5, 40.7: Macedon" — CONFIRMED (Pella 22.52E 40.76N). Macedon is a minor kingdom until Philip II (359); acceptable.

## Cross-era notes

1. Coastal labels: the three Phoenicia/Phoenician-cities points (35.4–35.5E on 33.7–33.8N) sit on the Lebanese shoreline; nudge each 0.1–0.15 degrees east.
2. The Ionian Greeks label (27, 37.9) is in the sea; use 27.6, 38.2.
3. "Achaemenid Persia" at 44, 34 labels Babylonia, not Persia. Rename or move.
4. Two labels are outright anachronistic for most of their era: "Neo-Assyrian Empire" on the Archaic map (Assyria fell 612) and "Egypt, a Persian satrapy" on the Classical map (Egypt independent 404–343).
5. The single most-repeated soft error in the slice is stating conventional inferred dates (545, 570, 381) as if fixed. Prefix them with "c." in the atlas text.
